# 3D Virtual Cinematography & Camera Engine

Every manga panel is a cinematic shot framed through a virtual camera lens. Understanding optics, focal lengths, and spatial layering is what separates master directors from amateur illustrators.

---

## 1. Virtual Lens Selection (Focal Length Optics)

### A. 14mm–24mm Ultra-Wide Lens (Fisheye Dynamic Distortion)
* **Optical Behavior:** Extreme barrel distortion, exaggerated perspective foreshortening. Elements closest to the lens appear gargantuan, while the background curves dramatically away.
* **When to Use:**
  - A fist or blade tip thrust directly toward the reader (fist appears larger than the character's entire torso).
  - High-speed parkour, diving from skyscrapers, or charging into battle.
  - Monstrous scale where the camera sits at the villain's foot, looking up at their silhouette reaching into the sky.

### B. 35mm–50mm Standard Lens (Natural Eye-Line)
* **Optical Behavior:** Zero artificial distortion. Proportions mimic the human eye.
* **When to Use:**
  - Grounded hand-to-hand martial arts grappling.
  - Conversational character interactions, emotional eye contact, and quiet dramatic moments.

### C. 85mm–200mm Telephoto Lens (Spatial Compression)
* **Optical Behavior:** Compresses depth. The background is pulled forward and feels right behind the subject. Shallow depth of field (blurred background).
* **When to Use:**
  - The Classic Western Standoff: Two duelists facing each other; the rival in the background looks enormous right behind the hero's shoulder.
  - Sniper rifle targeting, high-stakes ocular stare-downs, and isolated emotional sorrow.

## 2. Dynamic Camera Angles & Emotional Vectors

| Camera Angle | Technical Term | Psychological Impact on Reader |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Worm's-Eye (Low Angle)** | *Aori* (煽り) | Dominance, terror, god-like majesty, insurmountable authority. |
| **Bird's-Eye (High Angle)** | *Fukan* (俯瞰) | Vulnerability, despair, tactical entrapment, insignificant insect scale. |
| **Dutch Angle (Tilted Horizon)** | *Shatai* (斜体) | Panic, mental breakdown, spatial disorientation, rapid momentum shift. |
| **Over-the-Shoulder (OTS)** | *Kata-goshi* (肩越し) | Intense confrontational intimacy, interpersonal tension, eavesdropping. |
| **Point-of-View (POV)** | *Shisen* (視線) | Visceral terror. Reader sees through the victim's eyes right before a strike lands. |

## 3. The 3-Layer 3D Parallax Rule

Never draw a flat 2D panel where all characters sit on the same focal plane. Every key panel MUST compose across 3 distinct spatial layers:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ [Layer 1: FOREGROUND (Extreme Blur / Silhouette)]      │
│ - Over-the-shoulder edge, shattered blade fragment,   │
│   smoking muzzle, or dripping blood in front of camera │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [Layer 2: MIDGROUND (Pin-Sharp Focal Subject)]         │
│ - Protagonist executing the attack or reacting         │
│ - Master anatomical rendering and micro-expression     │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [Layer 3: BACKGROUND (Atmospheric Depth & Context)]    │
│ - Exploding pillars, shockwave dust clouds,            │
│   shattered moon, or terrified onlookers in soft focus │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```
This 3-layer architecture creates an illusion of tangible, three-dimensional depth within the flat page.
